# Pre-chewed results

Clarity is not bought with precision. Research mathematics stays whole here:
the null model, the statistic, the correction, the proof. What changes is the
order a reader meets them in. A reader who stopped doing statistics a year ago
must still read a result without remembering the method.

## A result

Every table or figure of numbers says, in this order:

1. **The question** it answers, in one sentence.
2. **How to read it**: what a cell is, which value is good, and what each
   region means when they differ (false alarms, power, side effects). A
   region that matters gets a colour or a box, not only a sentence.
3. **In short**: the one-line answer, with its numbers.

Prefer a figure to a table of more than a dozen numbers.

## A piece of mathematics

Layered, each layer complete in itself, none of them a simplification of the
next:

1. **A picture and a sentence**: what the object detects or describes.
2. **The exact statement**: the null, the statistic, the decision, with its
   symbols defined where they appear.
3. **Why it holds**: the derivation or the reference, for the reader who checks.

A statistical term (p-value, q-value, power) is explained in plain words where
it first appears on the page, never only linked.
